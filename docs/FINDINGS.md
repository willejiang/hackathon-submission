# Practice-set findings and what they changed

Nine practice cases, two architectures, one measurement layer. This records what
the runs exposed about *the harness*, because most of it was the harness's fault
rather than the model's.

## Results, rescored after the fixes

`scripts/score_outputs.py`, gating = bounding box, volume (2%), solids, surface
area (5%), genus, bore presence and bore depth, plus the pipeline's own projection
rule (mean orthographic IoU >= 0.93).

| case | volume / expected | area | ortho IoU | verdict | what was wrong |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1.0000 | ok | 0.994 | PASS | — |
| 2 | 1.0001 | ok | 0.988 | PASS | — |
| 3 | 1.0000 | ok | 0.989 | PASS | — |
| 4 | 0.9838 | ok | 0.975 | PASS | — |
| 5 | 0.9938 | **-7.5%** | 0.985 | **FAIL** | top-face pockets and all 12 tori absent |
| 6 | **2.5622** | -61.9% | 0.911 | **FAIL** | ran out of time, submitted a mid-construction solid |
| 7 | 1.0000 | -3.1% | **0.853** | **FAIL** | 88 faces against 628; every fillet family absent |
| 8 | 1.0057 | -11.6% | 0.934 | **FAIL** | genus 23 against 11 |
| 9 | 0.9988 | **-30.0%** | 0.987 | **FAIL** | bank bores 25.4 mm deep instead of 156.14 mm |

Every one of these passed the *old* gate set except case 6. That is the headline:
five parts were accepted while being wrong, and the gates had nothing to say.

## Finding 1 — silhouette IoU cannot see a recess

Measured directly: a 40 x 40 x 10 pocket in a 100 x 100 x 50 block removes 3.2% of
the volume and changes **zero pixels** in all eight silhouettes. A 20 x 20 x 5
pocket changes zero pixels *and* stays inside both the 2% volume and 3% area
tolerances, so it is invisible to the entire acceptance test.

The reason is structural, not a bug: a pocket cut into a face does not change that
face's outline. From any orthographic direction the projected material still fills
the same region. Shading reveals it in the reference render, but the automated
comparison only looks at silhouettes.

Case 5 is the real instance: 0.985 IoU, volume within 0.6%, and the three top-face
pockets and all twelve torus rounds missing.

**Fix.** Surface area became gating (5%), genus became gating, and `face_types`
now names any species sitting at 0 (`TORUS 12 declared`, `BSPLINE 12 declared`),
because "L1=75" does not tell a model that it omitted every round on the part.
Case 5 now fails on area.

A note on what is *not* fixed: the silhouette metric itself is unchanged. Adding a
depth buffer and comparing interior edges would catch pockets directly, and is the
right next step, but it is a bigger change than the evidence justified for this
round. Documented rather than pretended.

## Finding 2 — a case can end with no file

Case 6 hit the per-case timeout while still iterating and submitted nothing. A
missing file scores zero, which is strictly worse than submitting a mediocre one.

**Fix.** `run_agent.py` no longer trusts the agent to submit. If no STEP exists when
the run ends — timeout, crash, or a clean exit without `cad_submit` — it exports the
best module left in the workspace through `python -m agentic_cad.cli submit`. The
skill also now says *submit first, then improve*: get something that builds and
passes the gating facts, submit it, then keep going and submit again. Submitting
costs one tool call and can only help.

## Finding 3 — recesses and blind holes on the hard cases

The inputs are not the problem. Verified on all four: the briefs describe every
feature, with depths.

- case 5: six bores `d28 axis Y depth 7.62`, six coaxial `d6.35 depth 18.7-19.0`,
  `d5.11 depth 16.51`, three shallow rounded-rectangular pockets on the top face
- case 7: "top flange pocket ... rectangular recess open on the top face",
  `d29 (11.3 mm) recess` on the front face, counterbores `d65 (9 mm)` and
  `d68.5 (2 mm)`, keyway slot, and a full inventory of concave and convex fillets
  from R0.5 to R25
- case 8: "front-face blind holes ... blind to a depth of 4 mm", twelve `d12`
  half-cylinders "open to the top and bottom faces", "rectangular blind pocket"
- case 9: `16 x d9.52 blind depth 76.0`, six `d127` bank bores, `d127` transverse
  crankshaft slot

And `facts.json` carries the depths as `lengths` on every bore family. So the text
and the numbers were both sufficient. Three harness defects were in the way:

**(a) Oblique axes could never match.** facts.json writes an angled bore's axis as a
direction vector, `(0.0, 0.71, 0.71)`. The comparison compared it against the
measured label `"X"`/`"Y"`/`"Z"`. A letter never equals a vector, so the V6 block's
eight `d9.52` bores and the cylinder head's three `d12.7` bores were reported missing
*whatever the geometry actually was*. That is a false negative generated by the
verifier, and it wasted the agent's time on holes it may have modelled correctly.
Fixed: axes are compared as direction vectors, sign-insensitive, within about 10
degrees. On case 9 the `d9.52` families now match.

**(b) Depth was never compared.** The bore check matched on diameter and axis only.
A blind hole cut all the way through has the same diameter and the same axis, so it
passed. Case 8 passed its bore check with genus 23 against a declared 11 -- it had
definitely cut blind holes through, and nothing objected. Fixed: each measured
cylindrical face group now reports a total axial extent (so a bore split into two
coaxial faces still reports its real depth), and `hole_depths` compares it with the
declared `lengths` at 8%.

The payoff is the diagnostic quality. Case 9 used to report "missing 8x d9.52 along
(0, 0.71, 0.71)" — wrong, and unactionable. It now reports:

```
holes        d9.52 76.00/76.00mm ok; d127 25.40/156.14mm WRONG
hole_depths  d127: 25.40mm deep, facts say 156.14mm
genus        6 expected, 18 actual -- too many through passages usually means
             blind holes were cut all the way through
area         30.0% off -- usually missing surface features rather than wrong
             overall size; compare the face-type inventory
```

One sentence the agent can act on: the bank bores are too shallow. That also
explains the genus and the area, which is what a good diagnosis should do.

**(c) The checks that mattered were advisory.** Genus, surface area and the
face-type inventory were all non-gating, so a part could carry all three failures
and still be accepted. Genus and area are now gating. Face-type *counts* stay
advisory on purpose: reproducing 628 faces and 65 distinct tori is not a realistic
goal in the budget, and gating on it would just make every hard case red. What is
gating is the consequence — area and genus — and what is loud is the named species.

## Case 7 before and after the measurement step

The bracket is the hardest case: 628 declared faces, 16 bore families, 17
partial-arc families, 25 round families, 10 torus radii. Both runs used
`deepseek-flash` through the harness; the second had the model-written measurer and
the two plumbing fixes below.

| | architected run (old harness) | with the measurement step |
|---|---|---|
| wall clock | — | 1434 s (24 min) |
| volume / expected | 1.0000 | 1.0140 |
| mean ortho IoU | 0.8531 | **0.8718** |
| mean IoU, all views | 0.8619 | **0.8777** |
| genus (declared 10) | 14 | **10** |
| bore families matched | 6 of 16 | **15 of 16** |
| faces (declared 628) | 88 | **131** |
| surface area (declared 96750.8) | 93747, -3.1% | **99225, +2.6%** |
| gating failures | 10 bore families + projection | **1 bore family** + projection |
| comparison rows | 9, from the old hand-written list | **121**, every family in the brief |

The trade is visible: volume got slightly worse (1.4% high) and everything else got
better, because the loop finally told the agent which *features* were missing instead
of only that the total was close. Fifteen of sixteen bore families matched, and the
one still missing is the oblique `d4.5` on `(-0.29, -0.66, 0.70)`.

That is the argument for the measurement step in one line: a verifier that reports
totals teaches a model to chase totals.

## Two plumbing bugs the run exposed

Both had the same shape -- the harness knew something useful and delivered it as a
crash.

**`ProvenanceReport.render()` raised `TypeError`.** It did
`"\n".join(self.violations)` over a list of `Violation` objects. So *every* rejected
module came back to the model as a Python traceback instead of a rule. The bracket run
responded exactly as you would expect from a system that only says "something was
wrong": it wrote eight probe scripts (`p1.py` … `p8.py`, `probe.py`, `probe2.py`) to
discover the rule by experiment, and its reasoning shows it guessing. Fixed: violations
render with their source line, and a `build()` violation now says which numbers are
structural and that dimensions must pass through the parameter dict.

**Non-finite floats broke the tool channel.** A degenerate boolean leaves volume or
area as `NaN`, Python's `json` writes the bare token `NaN`, which is not JSON, and the
plugin's `JSON.parse` threw -- so the model saw `cli returned non-JSON` rather than
`your solid is degenerate`. Fixed with a recursive sanitiser. The agent had guessed
this one correctly on its own ("the error is on the tool result serialization"), which
is a good sign about the model and a bad sign about the harness.

## A risk this opened, not yet closed

The model-written measurer and the harness's built-in one **disagree**: scoring the
same STEP file, the built-in measurer reports three missing bore families, the model's
reports one. The model's grouping is more permissive -- it merges families that facts
keeps separate.

That is the cost of letting the model write the adapter. The structure check proves
the document has the right *families*; nothing proves it measured them *strictly*. A
measurer that merges everything into one family would pass the structure check and
report almost nothing.

The fix is to run both measurers where both can run and flag divergence, treating the
built-in one as the reference. Not implemented.


Of the three findings, only one is about model capability: on parts with hundreds
of features the model simplifies, drops fillets and stops early. That is real and
expected.

The other two were the harness failing to tell it what was wrong. A blind hole cut
through, a missing pocket, a bore on an oblique axis, a part that is 30% short on
surface — none of these produced a usable signal before. An agent cannot fix what
the verifier does not report, and the practice set showed it faithfully: the parts
that scored badly were the ones where the verifier was blind.

The ordering that follows: **make the verifier honest first, then ask more of the
model.** A stronger model on a verifier that cannot see recesses will still ship
parts with no recesses.

## Reproducing

```bash
python scripts/run_agent.py --timeout 900           # nine cases
python scripts/run_agent.py --cases 9 --timeout 60  # deliberately time out, watch the forced submit
python scripts/score_outputs.py --dir .runs/agent/outputs --verbose
```

`--verbose` prints the per-fact table and the per-view IoU, overfill and underfill
for each case, which is what the archived traces contain per round.
